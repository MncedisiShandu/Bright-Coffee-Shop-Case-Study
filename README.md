# Bright-Coffee-Shop-Case-Study
Bright Coffee Shop Sales Analysis

# 1. Overview of the Case Study

This case study focuses on analysing sales data from Bright Coffee Shop to understand business performance, customer purchasing patterns, product performance and revenue generation.

The main objective was to process and analyse the sales dataset to identify useful information that can help the business make informed decisions, improve sales performance and understand customer purchasing behaviour.

The dataset contains transaction-level sales information, including transaction dates, times, product categories, product types, store locations, quantities sold and unit prices.

# 2. Approach and Methodology

The case study was completed in two main stages: data processing and data visualisation.

# Task 1: Data Processing and SQL Analysis

Databricks was used to import, clean, transform and analyse the dataset.

The following activities were performed:

Imported the CSV dataset into Databricks.

Checked the dataset for missing values, duplicate records and invalid values.

Examined product categories, product types and store locations.

Created time buckets to group transactions into 30-minute intervals.

Converted unit prices from text to numeric values using CAST() and REPLACE().

Calculated total revenue for each transaction using unit price multiplied by transaction quantity.

Created additional columns to identify the day of the week, transaction month and whether a transaction occurred on a weekday or weekend.

Used SQL aggregation functions such as SUM(), COUNT() and GROUP BY to summarise sales performance.

# Task 2: Data Visualisation

Microsoft Excel was used to create PivotTables, charts and visual summaries to communicate the findings.

The visualisations focused on:

Sales performance by product category.

Total units sold and total revenue.

Revenue performance by store location and time of day.

Sales trends across different days and months.

Weekday versus weekend sales performance.

The relationship between total units sold and total revenue using a scatter plot.

# 3. Key Business Insights

The analysis was designed to provide insights into the following areas:

Product Performance: Identify product categories and individual products that contribute most to sales volume and revenue.

Store Performance: Compare revenue and sales activity across different store locations.

Sales Patterns: Identify busy periods throughout the day using 30-minute time intervals.

Daily and Monthly Trends: Understand how sales performance changes across different days of the week and months.

Revenue Contribution: Determine the percentage of total revenue generated by different product categories and products.

Sales Volume and Revenue Relationship: Examine whether higher quantities of products sold are associated with higher revenue.

These findings can help management understand sales performance, identify opportunities for improvement and support better business decisions.

# 4. Summary of Work Completed

The case study involved preparing the raw sales dataset, performing data quality checks, transforming data using SQL and developing visualisations in Excel.

The processed dataset included additional calculated fields and sales metrics, making it suitable for further analysis.

The analysis provided a structured view of product performance, store performance, revenue generation and customer purchasing patterns.

# 5. Tools and Technologies Used

The following tools were used throughout the case study: Databricks, SQL, Excel, Miro

# 6. Conclusion

This case study provided practical experience in data processing, SQL analysis and data visualisation.

It demonstrated how raw transactional data can be transformed into meaningful business information. The combination of Databricks and Excel made it possible to prepare, analyse and present sales information in a structured and understandable way.
